Understanding ISO Paper Sizes (A0–A5) & Print DPI

Every ISO “A” paper sheet shares the exact same √2 aspect ratio (1 : 1.4142), so halving one size produces the next smaller size — yet enlarging an image across formats reduces its pixel density dramatically.

What Is Effective Print DPI?

DPI (Dots Per Inch) — often used interchangeably with PPI (Pixels Per Inch) — measures how tightly your image’s pixels are packed onto physical paper. An image doesn’t have a fixed print quality until you decide how big you will print it.

Effective DPI = Image Pixels ÷ Printed Inches
🔍

Why Screens Look Sharp While Prints Blur

Web images and social media graphics are typically saved around 72–96 PPI because phone and laptop screens are backlit and viewed briefly. Paper reflects ambient light and reveals jagged “stair-step” edges and fuzzy text whenever pixel density drops below ~150 DPI at reading distance.

Green ≥ Safe DPI · Red < Safe DPI
👀

Viewing Distance Matters

An A5 postcard or A4 résumé is held 30 cm from the eyes, so 240–300 DPI keeps small text razor-sharp. A giant A1 or A0 wall poster is viewed from 1–2 meters away, so 100–150 DPI still looks smooth and vibrant to the human eye.

Closer Viewing = Higher DPI Needed

5 Practical Ways to Avoid Blurry Prints

  • 1 Design at your target size from the start: Set your canvas in Photoshop, Procreate, Figma, or Canva directly to 300 DPI at the final paper dimensions (e.g., 4961 × 7016 px for A2).
  • 2 Respect the Green-to-Red border: Whenever scaling up turns PeachyPrint’s border red, either scale back down to the suggested safe percentage or export a larger original file.
  • 3 Use vectors for text & logos: Text, icons, and line art stay infinitely sharp when exported from vector tools (Illustrator, Inkscape, Figma) at high resolution.
  • 4 Avoid screenshots or messaging downloads: Apps like WhatsApp and Instagram compress images heavily — always use the original uncompressed PNG, TIFF, or high-quality JPEG.
  • 5 Export true-size PDF for print shops: Sending a properly sized ISO PDF prevents automatic printer “stretch-to-fit” distortion and keeps margins predictable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens when I enlarge an A4 design onto an A2 poster?

A2 is exactly 2× wider and 2× taller than A4 (4× the area). Enlarging an A4 image to fill an A2 sheet cuts its effective DPI in half. If your A4 file was 300 DPI (2480 × 3508 px), it becomes 150 DPI on A2 — still acceptable for standard posters, whereas a 150 DPI A4 file drops to 75 DPI and will look noticeably pixelated (Red border).

Are my uploaded images sent to a server?

Never! PeachyPrint runs 100% locally inside your web browser using the HTML5 Canvas API. Your photos, designs, and exported PNG/PDF files never leave your computer or phone.

Does exporting at 300 DPI magically fix a low-resolution image?

No — exporting a 595 × 842 px image onto a 300 DPI A2 PDF creates a crisp container page, but the source artwork still only contains its original pixels. That is why PeachyPrint calculates effective DPI strictly from your source image’s real pixel count and warns you with a red border before you print.

Should I download PNG or PDF for my print shop?

Choose PDF (True ISO Size) when sending files to a copy shop or office printer so the exact physical millimeter size (such as 420 × 594 mm for A2) is embedded automatically. Choose PNG when you want a lossless raster image to import into another design app.
